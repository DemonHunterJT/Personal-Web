About

A researcher and designer bridging the gap between artificial intelligence and visual expression — with a guitar, a camera and a coffee cup never far away. Pull out the file to read more.

Jasmine Tse

Xie Yitong · AI Graduate · Guangzhou

I recently graduated from Sun Yat-sen University in School of Artificial Intelligence. My interests sit between Human-Computer Interaction and computer vision — at heart, I'm figuring out how technical logic can be translated into something warmer and more human.

When I'm not writing code, I'm the lead vocalist and guitarist of a band, a weekend café hunter, and a commissioned 3D modeller. I believe good design starts with curiosity — and curiosity mostly comes from living, not from papers.

Research

Bridging AI and Human Nuance

During my degree I worked on everything from predicting the intentions people express in Chinese online mental health communities (CHCHI '24), to semantic-level video reasoning segmentation at Tsinghua University (EMNLP 2026).

I like the parts nobody sees — building datasets, annotating raw material, and designing benchmarks that bridge algorithmic models with the ambiguity of the real world. What I care about most now is human-AI collaboration: systems that are useful, not merely clever.

Design & Craft

Where Logic Meets Hand

I've built a Blender add-on that detects mesh topology defects before 3D printing — turning detection output into repair suggestions a non-expert can actually follow. I've also led a team on a context-aware food recommendation prototype, and designed posters for the university music association.

Toolbox: Figma, Blender, the Adobe suite, a little Procreate, and a lingering obsession with type and mesh density. My approach is to start from what people actually do, not from what the model can do.

Off the Screen

Music, Cafés, Camera, Mesh

Music is a daily habit. I lead publicity for the SYSU Music Association, sing and play guitar in bands, and write songs nobody will ever hear.

Guangzhou is my weekend playground — I collect cafés, menu typefaces, and the way light falls on a windowsill. Café hunting is the hobby I do most.

Modelling is how I unwind: twenty-odd commissions, mostly small objects and scenes, mostly after a long day in the lab. I hold one simple creed — if something looks comfortable, it usually works comfortably too.
